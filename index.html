From download to desktop

Three steps. Then get on with it.

Chrome downloads the installer to Downloads, or the folder you choose. Installing is a simple drag into Applications.

01

Download & open

Download the Mac installer above. In Chrome's downloads list or Finder, open Screen-Shield-1.1.2-macOS-arm64.dmg.

02

Drag into Applications

In the disk image, drag Screen Shield onto Applications. Eject the disk image, then open the app from Applications.

03

Review & allow access

Read and accept the in-app Privacy Policy and Terms first. Then allow Camera and Screen Recording for Screen Shield. Accessibility / Input Monitoring enables the optional global shortcut. Relaunch after changing permissions.

Opening a student-built app on macOS

This is an experimental student project. Apple has not notarized this preview; student authorship does not prove safety. Only proceed if you trust this copy and its source.

  1. Move Screen Shield into Applications and try opening it once.
  2. If “Apple could not verify” appears, click Done.
  3. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security. Find the message about Screen Shield and select Open Anyway.
  4. Authenticate and confirm Open. Then review both policies in the app before accepting and enabling protection.

This is a per-app exception. Do not disable Gatekeeper globally. If macOS specifically reports malware, app damage, or a different warning, stop and check the source rather than treating it as this notarization notice. Apple’s instructions ↗

Replacing the version that crashed? Quit Screen Shield first. Drag version 1.1.2 into Applications and choose Replace. Don't reopen the old 1.0.0 copy from a previous download.

A quiet layer of privacy

It watches for another face.
You keep working.

Only a nearby, centrally positioned face qualifies as the owner, with position tracking to keep that assignment stable. If nobody qualifies, every detected face is a candidate viewer. Other faces must be close enough, face roughly toward the display, and stay long enough before a cover appears.

The app captures and blurs the primary screen once, then holds that frozen image until the viewer leaves. Unknown or dark camera frames hold the current state. A small control window keeps enable, snooze, and quit within reach. Optional Model view shows the camera with green owner, yellow other-face, and red viewer-candidate boxes. The privacy cover stays off while testing; closing the view restores your previous setting.

A privacy aid, not a guarantee: it cannot spot people outside the camera's view, detect a phone filming your screen, or stop software screenshots. Head direction is not eye gaze.

Before you install

A few things
worth knowing.

Why might macOS block the download?

This is an ad-hoc-signed preview, not a Developer ID-signed or Apple-notarized release. Internet downloads can be blocked by Gatekeeper. A smooth public release still needs Apple's signing and notarization process. Do not disable Gatekeeper globally. Apple's distribution guidance ↗

What changed in the crash-fix release?

The original crash fix moved macOS keyboard-layout lookup onto the main thread before the global-hotkey listener starts. Version 1.1.2 also relaxes the primary-user distance threshold and stabilizes that assignment when leaning back.

Does the app record or upload my camera?

No. Camera frames and the blurred screenshot are processed in memory on your Mac. Screen Shield does not save or transmit them. This page uses no analytics, remote fonts, or camera access.

Which Macs are supported?

This build is for Apple Silicon Macs (M1 or newer) running macOS 15 or later. It isn't an Intel, Windows, or browser-extension download. Live camera accuracy and display behavior depend on your setup.

Can I change the sensitivity?

Yes. Preferences live at ~/Library/Application Support/Screen Shield/config.json. Thresholds control face size, head angle, and trigger/clear timing. Restart the app after editing. No benchmark accuracy is claimed.

Is there another download format?

You can use the ZIP archive instead. Extract it and move Screen Shield.app into Applications. SHA-256 checksums are available for both files.
